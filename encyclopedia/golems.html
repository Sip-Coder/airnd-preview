Golems

In Jewish folklore a golem is an unformed body, often clay, animated by language and set to a task.

I start the class with a golem because the story is older than any lab and it already knows the punchline. A golem is unfinished matter. In the famous Prague telling, the rabbi shapes clay into a servant for the community. The story is legend, not a lab report, and that is fine. Legends are where people store the warnings they do not want to lose.

The word itself is older than that city tale. In Jewish sources a golem can mean something unformed. There is a tradition that even the first human was golem-like before speech. So the fantasy is not only 'build a robot.' It is 'shape something that does not yet have a voice, then give it one.'

How the animation works changes by the telling. Often it is letters. Sometimes a word placed on the forehead, or a name written and later erased. The power is language. You do not plug the clay in. You tell it, carefully, what it is.

And then the story turns. The servant does the task too well, or the task was sloppy, or nobody watches it. Some versions end with dust, water, or a community that has to shut the thing back into mud. I want you to hear that before you hear the word 'prompt.' A made worker follows the instruction it was given, including the version you did not mean.

When we leave this node, keep one picture: a person, a lump of clay, and a sentence that has to be good enough to live with. Next is another made body, this one bronze, hired to walk a shoreline.
